The Republic of Finland ( Swedish: Republiken Finland ) or Finland (Swedish: Finland ) is a parliamentary republic in Northern Europe . Finland is one of the Nordic countries , and geographically Finland is part of the Fennoscandian peninsula and one of the Baltic Sea littoral states. Finland's neighbors are Russia to the east , Norway to the north , and Sweden to the west . To the west is the Gulf of Bothnia and to the south is the Gulf of Finland , with Estonia on the opposite shore .

Finland is located in the northern coniferous forest zone , and the country has over 168,000 lakes . Finland's winters are cold and its summers are relatively mild. Finland has a population of 5.6 million , the majority of whom are Finnish . The capital and largest city is Helsinki . The official languages ​​are Finnish and Swedish , spoken as a native language by 84.1 percent and 5.3 percent of the population, respectively.

Finland was settled shortly after the end of the last ice age , around 9,000 BC. The Baltic Finnic languages ​​diverged by the beginning of the Common Era. Crusades were launched in Finland from the 12th century onwards. The region was for a long time part of Sweden , until in 1809 Finland became part of Russia after the Finnish War . Finland gained independence in 1917. In 1918 the division of the people led to a civil war . In 1919 a republican form of government was confirmed . During World War II, Finland fought the Winter War , the Continuation War and the Lapland War , but retained its independence. Finland was for a long time an agricultural country, but from the 1950s onwards rapid industrialisation and urbanisation began .

Finland joined the European Union in 1995, adopted the euro in 2002, and joined NATO in 2023. Finland is a welfare state with a high gross domestic product per capita . Finland has ranked highly in several international comparisons , including civil liberties, quality of life, and human development . [ 10 ] Finland has been ranked the happiest country in the world in a report by the United Nations Sustainable Development Network since 2018. [ 11 ]

Name
Main article : Finnish names
The origin of the name Suomi is unknown. However, the name can be shown to be old: it is e -bodied, and such bodies, with a few exceptions, are at least one and a half thousand years old. [ 12 ] Before its meaning expanded, Suomi referred to the area around Turku , which is now called Southwest Finland , and the language of its Finnish-speaking population; it is unclear whether the word was originally a place name or an ethnonym . [ 13 ] [ 12 ] [ 14 ] The oldest surviving mention is from 811, when a person named Suomi is mentioned in the annals of the Frankish Empire ; the word appears as a place name, among others, in the town of Somewesi in 1323. [ 13 ]

Old explanations, in which the name is derived from suo , suomu or suoda , have been rejected on both etymological and semantic grounds. [ 13 ] Jorma Koivulehto proposed in 1993 that Finnish and Sámi are ultimately the same word: the Baltic word *žemē meaning 'land' would have passed through Proto-Finnish and Proto- Sámi as a loanword back to Baltic and from there to Baltic Finnish in the form *sōme , in which case the provincial name Häme also belongs to the same connection. [ 15 ] [ 13 ] Petri Kallio derived the name in 1998 independently of this from an Indo-European word meaning 'land' or 'human'. [ 16 ] Kallio's etymology was for a long time the most widely accepted, but he himself has since rejected it. [ 17 ] According to a more recent understanding, both Finnish and the Sámi sápmi go back to the Finno-Sámi root word *sämä , whose own origin is unknown. [ 13 ] [ 12 ]

Most languages ​​in the world use the name Finland for Finland , which is based on the Old Norse element finnr . The word meant different peoples in different Scandinavian languages: in Norwegian it referred to the Sámi and Lapland , in Swedish to the inhabitants of Southwest Finland. [ 18 ] In Norway , finne and Finnmarken still refer to the Sámi and their homeland today; when the Swedes began to call the Sámi Lapps, the word became established for the Finnish-speaking inhabitants of Finland. [ 18 ] This seems to have been a general term for the Scandinavians to their northern neighbours, meaning roughly 'those who are not us' – similar to the Germanic word from which the names Welsh , Walloon and Vlach originate . As Swedish rule expanded eastwards in the late Middle Ages, the meanings of Finland and Finland expanded hand in hand. [ 19 ]

The Latin name Finlandia began to appear in the titulary of the bishops of Turku from 1221 and referred to the southwestern provinces around Turku; from 1308 the eastern regions were called Österlandet or Itämaa . [ 20 ] From 1464 and especially after 1500 , Finland referred to the entire region, and Österlandet last appeared in official documents in 1525. [ 20 ] [ 21 ] John III assumed the title of Grand Duke of Finland in 1581, and from 1809 the Grand Duchy of Finland was the official name of autonomous Finland within the Russian Empire . [ 22 ]
